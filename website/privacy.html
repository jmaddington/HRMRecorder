Privacy.

Effective July 12, 2026. Last updated July 12, 2026.

HRM Recorder is built so that your heart-rate data stays on your iPhone, under your control. No accounts. No analytics. No third-party SDKs. The following sections describe exactly what the app does — and what it deliberately does not do — with your data.

What HRM Recorder stores

When you record a session, the app writes every Heart Rate Measurement notification it receives from your Bluetooth strap to a SQLite database inside the app's sandbox on your iPhone. Each sample includes:

  • A timestamp
  • The reported beats-per-minute value
  • Any R-R intervals included in the same packet
  • Sensor contact and energy-expended fields, when the strap reports them
  • The identifier of the strap that produced the reading

For each strap you connect, the app stores its advertised Bluetooth name and the manufacturer / model / firmware identifiers the strap reports via the standard Device Information Service. This is what makes each exported CSV self-describing.

What HRM Recorder does not collect

  • No account, email address, or other identifier — there is no sign-up.
  • No analytics, usage tracking, or telemetry.
  • No third-party SDKs of any kind. The app uses only Apple frameworks plus the system SQLite library.
  • No location, GPS, motion, microphone, camera, contacts, calendar, or photos. None of those permissions are requested.
  • No advertising identifier (IDFA). The app does not display ads.
  • No HealthKit access. The app does not read from or write to Apple Health.

What permissions the app asks for

Just one: Bluetooth. iOS requires the app to ask for permission to scan for and connect to your heart-rate strap; the permission prompt appears on first launch. The app uses Bluetooth solely to connect to a heart-rate sensor of your choosing and receive its readings.

The app also declares the bluetooth-central background mode, so that recording continues with the screen off and so that iOS can wake the app on a strap event after the system has suspended it. No other background modes are used.

When data leaves your phone

Your heart-rate data only leaves your iPhone in one of two ways, and only when you initiate it:

  • CSV export. Tapping export hands a CSV file to the iOS share sheet. Where you send it from there — Files, AirDrop, email, a cloud drive — is your choice and is governed by whichever service you pick.
  • Optional self-hosted sync. The app can optionally upload sessions to a server endpoint you configure. There is no built-in cloud, no managed service, no default destination. If you choose to use sync, the endpoint URL and any credentials live on your device only; the developer never sees them.

Crash reports and diagnostics

The app does not include a crash-reporting SDK or analytics SDK. If you opt in to Apple's system-level "Share With App Developers" setting, Apple may share aggregated, anonymized crash and diagnostic reports with the developer through App Store Connect. That sharing is governed by Apple's privacy policy and is entirely under your control in iOS Settings → Privacy & Security → Analytics & Improvements.

Children

The app is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect any information from anyone. Since the app does not collect information from anyone, this point is largely academic — but worth stating.

Medical disclaimer

HRM Recorder is not a medical device. It is not designed, intended, or authorized to diagnose, monitor, treat, cure, or prevent any disease or condition. The data it records reflects what your Bluetooth strap reports and should not be used as a substitute for clinical-grade monitoring or medical advice. Always talk to a qualified clinician about your health.

Changes to this policy

If this policy ever changes in a way that affects how the app handles your data, the change will be reflected here, the "Last updated" date above will move forward, and a note will appear in the app's release notes.
